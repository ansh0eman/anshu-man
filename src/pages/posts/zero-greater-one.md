I have `0 > 1` tattooed on my wrist.

It is small enough that people usually assume it is maths. It is not. I was never trying to win an argument against numbers. One is perfectly fine. Reliable, even. It knows exactly what it is.

That is kind of the problem.

Zero is nothing. Not “nothing” as in boring or empty or sad. Nothing with a capital N. No fixed shape. No assigned purpose.

A one will always be a one. It can join other numbers, become part of something larger, maybe make a two or a fifty-four or whatever. But it begins as itself. It arrives with a job title.

Zero gets to be filled.

It can become anything, or it can stay empty for a while. It has no moral scorecard, no cosmic checklist, no little voice from the universe telling it that it has failed the assignment. Which is probably why I think zero is greater than one.

Not mathematically. Chill out, dawg.

I like nihilism for this reason. Not because I think nothing matters, or because I want to sit in a dark room explaining why birthdays are fake. That sounds exhausting. Nihilism does not hand you meaning; it just removes the strange pressure to believe meaning was pre-installed somewhere.

It lets you clear the table.

Once you accept that there might not be one grand point to all of this, you get to notice the smaller points you would have otherwise walked past. Reading something that changes how you look at a day. A song that gets stuck in your head at the exact right time. Going somewhere new. Having dinner with family. Meeting someone who makes the whole world feel slightly less random for a bit.

None of those things have to matter to everyone.

They matter to me. That is enough.

The tattoo is not a reminder that life is empty. It is a reminder that empty is not the same as useless. An empty page can become a story. A blank project folder can become something ridiculous and real. A day with no plan can become the day you find a book, a place, a person, or an idea you did not know you needed.

I do not think nihilism gives answers. I think it gives you permission to stop accepting the bad ones.

So yes, `0 > 1`.

Not because nothing beats everything.

Because nothing leaves room for anything.
